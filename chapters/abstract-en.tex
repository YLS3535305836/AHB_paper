With the rapid development of global integrated circuit technology, the intelligence level of portable electronic devices continues to improve, leading to increasing demands for charging power and speed in fast-charging technology. Flyback AC/DC switching power supplies are widely used in low-to-medium power applications due to their excellent isolation performance, simple design, and low cost. However, their low light-load efficiency and limited switching frequency hinder the development of high power density, making them unable to meet the growing social demand. To explore more possibilities of flyback converters, this paper designs a flyback converter chip based on an asymmetric half-bridge topology, applied in the fast-charging field for medium-power environments.

To address the aforementioned issues, this paper optimizes the traditional flyback converter’s shortcomings of high light-load losses and low output power by designing an asymmetric half-bridge flyback converter based on a secondary-side feedback structure. To fully leverage the advantages of the asymmetric half-bridge flyback converter, its topology is systematically studied, and a series of control strategies and key circuit modules are proposed to achieve high-frequency and high-efficiency operation over a wide output voltage and full load range. In extremely light-load and no-load conditions, unnecessary modules are shut down through burst mode to reduce standby power consumption. Under light load, a zero-voltage resonant valley switching (ZV-RVS) mode is adopted, introducing reverse magnetization current to achieve soft-switching for power devices. Under heavy load, a critical conduction mode is used to control asymmetric complementary conduction of power switches to meet high output power demands. Additionally, a valley-locking circuit is designed to eliminate frequency hopping issues in the power overlap range, and a peak current control circuit is incorporated to achieve a correspondence between secondary-side feedback voltage and load current under a wide output range, meeting multi-mode control requirements.

Furthermore, to further explore the potential of the asymmetric half-bridge flyback system in high power density, high conversion efficiency, and high reliability, the following key innovations have been made:

To further optimize system efficiency in ZV-RVS mode, a precise valley conduction technique is proposed to reduce switching losses and current spikes caused by resonance voltage effects. This technique improves upon quasi-resonant technology by adopting a more precise valley detection scheme, eliminating the delay error introduced by the driver circuit through phase lead and approach strategies, reducing it to within ±15 ns, and achieving accurate conduction of power switches at the lowest point of the resonance valley.

For the high-efficiency control strategy of the asymmetric half-bridge flyback converter, a demagnetization time dynamic calibration technique is proposed. By using a dynamically compensated integral slope circuit to control the energy transfer time between the transformer’s primary and secondary sides, the slope voltage rate is adjusted to gradually overlap with the demagnetization completion time of the magnetizing inductance. This avoids increased conduction losses and reliability issues caused by excessively long or short energy transfer times, thereby meeting the high-efficiency requirements of multiple control modes.

The designed converter chip is developed based on SMIC 0.18μm BCD process technology and operates within an input range of 90-264Vac, with a rated output range of 5-20V and a load current range of 0-5A. Simulation results show that under multi-mode control strategies and various key technologies, the circuit effectively improves system conversion efficiency at a maximum output power of 100W, achieving a peak conversion efficiency of 90\% and a load regulation rate of 0.8\%, meeting performance requirements.
